# Joke candidates — selection notes

Judge brief (from the published rules): a genuine one-line office joke,
a real turn at the end, recognition that makes tired office workers wince,
tight wording, and freshness — not the obvious Monday-morning or
photocopier line. Every candidate below was written fresh in this session
and scored against those five criteria, most important first.

## Rejected

1. "My performance review said I 'exceed expectations'; my raise was 2%,
   which exceeds my expectations of ever affording rent."
   Good wordplay turn, strong wince — but the tiny-raise premise is one of
   the most common office jokes in existence. Loses on freshness.
2. "HR's happiness survey had one option for 'very unhappy': 'please
   discuss this with your manager.'"
   Sharp turn, tight (100 chars). Kept as runner-up; the premise is
   strong but the laugh is a smirk, not a room laughing.
3. "Our office plant has survived three rounds of layoffs, two CEOs, and
   one fire — HR just gave it a performance review and a raise."
   Fresh premise, but the turn lands soft: a plant getting a review is
   absurd rather than surprising in a joke-shaped way.
4. "I labelled my lunch in the office fridge — someone ate it and left a
   note: 'Thanks for sharing! Let's circle back on the leftovers.'"
   Good recognition, but the fridge-theft setup is familiar and the line
   runs long for its punch.
5. "IT closed my printer ticket as 'resolved' — the printer still doesn't
   print, but now it's officially not a problem."
   The brief itself names the printer as a recognition example, so a
   printer joke is exactly what hundreds of entries will send. Rejected
   on freshness.
6. "My calendar is so full that my lunch break now has a waitlist, and the
   waitlist has a meeting to discuss the waitlist."
   Decent escalation, but "calendar full" is a well-worn premise and the
   turn is a repeat of the setup rather than a new place.
7. "Our 'unlimited PTO' policy is amazing — I've taken zero days off in
   two years and management calls me a role model."
   True and wince-inducing, but the unlimited-PTO paradox is a known
   trope; the turn is predictable once the setup is read.

## Winner

"The fire drill was the only meeting this quarter where everyone arrived
on time, left early, and nobody asked to circle back."

Why it wins: the premise is unusual — nobody writes the fire drill as the
office's best-run meeting, so it is fresh against a field of Monday and
printer jokes. The turn is structural: an emergency evacuation beats every
real meeting on every metric a meeting is judged by, and the final beat
("nobody asked to circle back") lands the corporate jargon where it hurts.
Recognition is immediate — punctuality, leaving early, circling back — and
"left early" quietly does double duty (left the meeting early / left the
building). 125 characters, every word earning its place.
